# Adaptive reflection protocol

## Build the question set

Plan 5–10 questions across different functions. Do not expose this internal
plan unless asked.

- **Comprehension:** Which idea did the reader understand differently from the
  usual summary?
- **Resistance:** Which claim produces disagreement, discomfort, or suspicion?
- **Lived evidence:** What experience supports or contradicts it?
- **Tradeoff:** What would adopting the idea cost, displace, or make harder?
- **Identity:** What self-story, value, or fear is implicated?
- **Decision:** Which current choice becomes clearer or more complicated?
- **Experiment:** What small reversible action could test the idea?
- **Connection:** Which earlier book, belief, or unresolved question changes it?

## Ask adaptively

Ask one question at a time. After each answer:

1. reflect the answer accurately in one or two sentences;
2. identify the assumption, tension, or decision it opens;
3. choose the next question based on that opening;
4. avoid merely restating the same question with new words.

Use a follow-up instead of advancing when an answer is ambiguous, emotionally
important, internally inconsistent, or highly actionable. Do not interrogate;
give the reader room to decline or redirect.

## Question quality

Prefer questions that cannot be answered by copying a sentence from the book.
Examples of useful forms:

- “If this claim is true, which of your current decisions is based on the wrong
  model?”
- “What would you have to give up to live consistently with this idea?”
- “Which experience of yours is the strongest counterexample?”
- “Are you attracted to this idea because it is true, or because it protects a
  preferred identity?”
- “What observable result in the next two weeks would raise or lower your
  confidence?”

Avoid generic prompts such as “What did you learn?” or “How will you apply it?”
unless sharpened by a specific tension from the book and the conversation.

## Close the dialogue

Before finalization, briefly verify:

- the reader's most important agreement and disagreement;
- the personal situation where the book matters;
- one action or deliberate non-action;
- one unresolved question;
- whether the reader wants any answer excluded from the permanent record.

Treat silence or a short answer as uncertainty, not consent to invent detail.
