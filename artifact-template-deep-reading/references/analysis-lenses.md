# Deep-reading lenses

Use the smallest set of lenses that reveals the book's structure. Do not turn
the result into a checklist.

## Evidence ladder

For every important conclusion, distinguish:

1. **Textual claim** — what the author explicitly argues, with a location.
2. **Reconstruction** — the implicit premise or mechanism needed for the
   argument to work.
3. **Evaluation** — why the argument is strong, weak, incomplete, or conditional.
4. **Reader relevance** — what changes for this reader, grounded in dialogue.

Use compact quotations only when wording itself matters. Prefer paraphrase plus
location. Never fabricate page numbers; use chapter or section when pagination
is unavailable.

## Structural lenses

- **Real problem:** What difficulty is the book actually trying to solve beneath
  its stated topic?
- **Argument map:** Which premises, mechanisms, examples, and conclusions depend
  on one another?
- **Load-bearing concepts:** Which few ideas explain the most, and how are they
  different from neighboring concepts?
- **World model:** What does the author assume about human nature, knowledge,
  power, freedom, time, value, institutions, or causality?
- **Philosophical tension:** Which goods or truths cannot be maximized together?
- **Negative space:** What is missing, silenced, treated as fixed, or excluded by
  the framing?
- **Steelman and falsifier:** What is the strongest version of the argument, and
  what evidence would materially weaken it?
- **Boundary:** Where does the idea stop working—population, culture, time,
  incentives, scale, or moral premise?
- **Transfer:** What decision, habit, relationship, work problem, or identity
  question could the idea change?

## Quality test

A deep insight should do at least two of the following:

- compress several parts of the book into one explanatory mechanism;
- reveal a hidden premise or tradeoff;
- survive a strong counterexample;
- distinguish a seductive misuse from the author's actual claim;
- change a prediction or decision;
- connect to the reader's lived evidence without forcing an analogy.

Reject generic statements that could describe hundreds of books. Reject praise
that lacks a mechanism. Reject criticism that does not first state the author's
best case.
